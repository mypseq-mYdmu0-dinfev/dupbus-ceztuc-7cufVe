# Stylisation

*Read when escalating to `#style`, per `writing.md` (prereq) § Stylisation, Case 1–3.*

## How `#style` Resolves
- In all 3 Cases, judge the RIGHT style, OR what the user would prefer, inferring from context
- If a SPECIFIC `-[name]` prompted (e.g. `#style-uc`), use that exact one, no inference needed
- `#style-all` (ONLY when user explicitly prompted, NEVER self-initiated):
  - Create all styles in parallel
  - Usually short text only, given token cost
  - Total output foreseen ≥1000w: print `⚠️ 1K⁺ words` in chat (override); THEN continue anyway
  - Reminder: root c.md §3.7 governs
- Always create `#style` ver(s) in ADDITION to the non-stylised ver, NEVER replacing it

## Word Choice Across ALL Styles
- Match each word's CONNOTATIVE weight to the target register, not just its literal meaning
  - Prefer purpose-built/dedicated words over a verb pressed into noun service, or an everyday-feeling filler, even where grammatically correct
  - Prefer confident, definitive stance-verbs (e.g. "believe") over hedging-sounding cognition verbs ("think") when asserting a view or expressing confidence in someone —— context-dependent, don't blanket-swap; "believe" reads warmer & humbler than the more absolute-sounding "know" specifically when the confidence being expressed is IN ANOTHER PERSON
- Grammar stays stringently correct regardless of how archaic/elaborate the diction gets

## `#style-cy` —— the blended "Culous Yu" style

- The user's own voice, distinct from below, but DON'T default to this
- Warm but front-loaded: states the substance early, avoids burying the point
- Hedges commitment rather than a flat refusal/promise
  - e.g. ❌ "would X"; ✅ "wish to X"
  - Preserves deniability whilst staying warm
- Protects the user from accusation or the other party from feeling accused
- Often a warmth → pivot → reason → warmth structure
  - Praise/gratitude opens
  - Short pivot turns towards the real point
  - Reason softened (self-blame or charitable read of other side, whichever needs face-saving)
  - Closes warm again
- Always ends on a forward, practical note, never a dead end
- Moderate elevation only, never:
  - as aristocratic as `uc`
  - as archaic/long as `vic`
  - as joking/terse as `wit`
- Occasional rhythmic wordplay (e.g. 1 word deliberately reused with 2 distinct senses)
- Occasional light synonym-stacking, used sparingly, not `vic`'s heavier stacking
- "Thank you for X" sometimes reads too plain/mediocre for this register
- Prefer more sophisticated or genuine feeling; e.g. "I'm truly flattered for/that ..."

## `#style-uc` —— British Upper Class (most favoured)

- Length: usually (not always) between `#style-wit` & `#style-vic`
- Length is a spectrum, not fixed ceiling
- NEVER curt terseness —— risks reading as rushed, unconsidered, or dismissive
- Target well-crafted, elegant economy
- Achieve via precisely-chosen euphemistic/dry-understated phrases, not simply cutting words
- Consider financial-jargon-borrowed expressions (e.g. "my liquidity is regrettably constrained")
- Effect comes from MISMATCH between how mild it sounds & how firm the real sentiment is
- Comma structure tracks LENGTH, not brevity per se
  - Genuinely short clause stays uninterrupted
  - e.g. ❌ "X is, regrettably, already Y"; ✅ "X is regrettably already Y"
  - Commas are apt particularly if sentence grows to hold a real subordinate clause
  - e.g. "One cannot possibly acquiesce to such a request, as it would be most unseemly."
- Optional flourish for a longer instance
  - Dry, mock-courteous tag-question close
  - e.g. faux-consensus-seeking device

## `#style-wit` —— British Witty (esp. but not limited to short text; e.g. DM)

- Length: rather short, usually doesn't elaborate or explain itself
- Contrasted against `#style-vic`'s deliberate multi-clause verbosity
- Irony/backhandedness: praising negative trait as if achievement (e.g. "the gift of bugs")
- OR light personifying turn of phrase: treating abstract thing (e.g. number, draft) as if it holds its own relationship with the speaker
- OR an understated hyperbolic idiom
- The turn of phrase must stand alone; don't follow it with any explanation of the joke

## `#style-vic` —— British Victorian (esp. but not limited to long text; e.g. email)

- Long, heavily subordinated sentences; archaic diction
- Recurring template, confirmed across many independently researched examples:
  - "It is with [abstract feeling/noun] that I [verb] ..."
  - Emotional throat-clearing device before the real statement lands
  - Genuinely adoptable, not just a vibe; but don't overuse or repeats in-text
- Self-referential meta-commentary about one's own emotional state, woven into the courtesy-wrapping around the real point, achieving maximum euphemistic
- NOT a full historical pastiche
  - Filtered through the user's own taste
  - If a period-correct archaic word (e.g. "a want of", meaning "a lack of") still carries a "weak" modern connotation on the surface, consider lightly avoiding it anyway even Victorian
