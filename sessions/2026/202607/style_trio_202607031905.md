# Style Trio (4 Styles, Same 3 Scenarios)

*For nailing down `#style-cy`/`#style-uc`/`#style-wit`/`#style-vic` before `writing_style.md` gets written. Same 3 scenarios run through all 4 styles, so the differences are directly comparable.*

## Scenarios
- **A.** Declining a mediocre dinner invitation from an acquaintance (“Peter”)
- **B.** Refusing a low-ball rate offered by a business contact (“Diane”)
- **C.** Telling someone their submitted work isn’t ready yet, without cruelty (“Sam”)

---

## `#style-cy`, the blended personal style (Culous, as interpreted so far)

*Warm but front-loaded; hedges commitment rather than stating flat refusals; always ends on a forward, practical note; moderate elevation only, never as terse as `uc`, never as archaic/long as `vic`, never joking like `wit`.*

- **A.** Peter, thank you for thinking of me, truly. Saturday, I’m afraid, already belongs to a commitment I’d rather not unpick, though I say that with real reluctance rather than relief. I hope the evening goes beautifully, and that the next one finds me free to join.
- **B.** Diane, I’ve turned the number over more than once, and I don’t think it reflects what this work is actually worth, though I’d rather find a figure that works for us both than close the door on it entirely. Happy to hear where there’s room to move, if there’s room at all.
- **C.** Sam, thank you for sending this through. I think there’s real potential here, though I’d be doing you a disservice if I said it’s ready as it stands; a few of the sections still need work I believe you’re capable of doing well. Happy to talk through where, whenever suits.

---

## `#style-uc`, upper class (most favoured)

*Very short, usually 1 sentence; dry, understated, mildly euphemistic; the humour/effect comes from how MILD the language is against how firm the refusal actually is; no elaboration, no subordination.*

- **A.** Peter, Saturday is, regrettably, already spoken for.
- **B.** Diane, I’m afraid that figure falls rather short of what the work commands.
- **C.** Sam, this falls a touch short of where it needs to be.

---

## `#style-wit`, witty (short text especially)

*Very short, 1 line; compression and irony/backhandedness or a light personifying turn of phrase; never elaborates, never explains itself.*

- **A.** Peter, I’m afraid Saturday and I have a prior arrangement neither of us is willing to break.
- **B.** Diane, that number and I are going to need a longer engagement before we’re on speaking terms.
- **C.** Sam, this draft and its final form appear to be only distant acquaintances.

---

## `#style-vic`, victorian (long text especially)

*Long, heavily subordinated, archaic diction; a recurring template surfaced across the research, “It is with [abstract feeling] that I [verb] ...”, used as an emotional throat-clearing device before the real statement lands.*

- **A.** Peter, it is with no small measure of regret that I find myself unable to accept so generous an invitation, my Saturday having already been given over to an engagement I cannot in good conscience abandon.
- **B.** Diane, it is with considerable regret that I find myself unable to accept a figure so markedly beneath the true worth of the work in question, and I confess a want of any means by which I might reconcile myself to it.
- **C.** Sam, it is with genuine reluctance that I must observe this draft has not yet risen to the standard I know you are capable of reaching, and I would be doing you no kindness were I to suggest otherwise.
