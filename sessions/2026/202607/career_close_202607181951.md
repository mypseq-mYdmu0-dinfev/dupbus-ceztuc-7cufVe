## 6. Remarks —— Learnings
- 6.1. **Reconstruction-from-notes beats reconstruction-from-captions alone.** The 3-SA garbled-transcript reconstruction held up well on its own, but the user's contemporaneous notes (supplied AFTER, not before) resolved the residual uncertainty far faster than further inference passes would have —— worth requesting rough live notes UPFRONT for future `#int` post-interview work, not relying on recordings/captions alone.
- 6.2. **A defined-but-unused notation reads as confusing, not harmless.** `[unclear]` was specified as a fallback tag but never actually triggered (every span was recoverable enough for `[best guess?]`); the user twice suspected data loss because a tag they expected to see never appeared. State explicitly, inside the artefact itself, when a defined marker goes unused, not only in the `response_`.
- 6.3. **Soft-judgement words benefit from a hard operational definition before use.** The user's bar-not-degree framework for "professional" (a role either clears a standard or does not, independent of quality or attitude) turned a fuzzy impression into a decisive, defensible verdict once a broken commitment was measured against it —— a reusable pattern for future character reads.
- 6.4. **Word-of-mouth is a trap strength.** High conversion, near-zero scalability —— worth checking explicitly whenever a small firm's growth story leans on WOM, since it silently caps the very upside a role promises to unlock.
- 6.5. **Retain-vs-remove is not simply "if it's now moot, delete it".** The user kept 2 of 3 SA-flagged-for-removal sections specifically because they will be read narrowly under a future `#int` protocol, not swept up in a whole-AR pass —— worth checking planned FUTURE read-patterns before trimming, not only current relevance.
