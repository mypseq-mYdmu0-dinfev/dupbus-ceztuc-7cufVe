**Participant Information Sheet**

**01. Title of Study: Tall Poppy Syndrome as a Management Challenge in Knowledge-Based Firms**

**02. Version and date:** Version 1, 4 July 2026

**03. Invitation to participate in a research study**

> You are being invited to participate in a research study. Before you decide whether to participate, it is important for you to understand why the research is being done and what it will involve. Please take time to read the following information carefully and feel free to ask us if you would like more information or if there is anything that you do not understand. Please also feel free to discuss this with your friends, relatives and family doctor if you wish. We would like to stress that you do not have to accept this invitation and should only agree to take part if you want to.
>
> Thank you for reading this

**04. What is the purpose of the study?**

> This research aims to investigate how Tall Poppy Syndrome influences team cohesion and knowledge governance in Australian Knowledge-Based Firms, crystallising the empirical foundation for CYC’s knowledge management consulting practice.

**05. Why have I been chosen to take part?**

> You have been invited because you have professional experience working within a knowledge-based firm in Australia, and have observed how a team responds when a colleague stands out as a strong performer. Participants are approached individually, and no more than one person is recruited from any single organisation.

**06. Do I have to take part?**

> No. Your participation is entirely voluntary. You can choose whether or not to take part. Even if you choose to take part, you can change your mind and you are free to withdraw at any time without giving any explanation. There will be no negative impact or disadvantage to you if you choose not to take part, nor if you change your mind and choose to withdraw.

**07. What will happen if I take part?**

> If you choose to take part in the study then you will be interviewed once by the researcher, who is a University of Liverpool Master’s student called Culous Yu. You will be asked around 10-15 open ended questions by the researcher. The questions will be about your experiences of teamwork, collaboration, and knowledge sharing when working alongside standout performers. This should take about 45-60 minutes of your time. The interview will be audio-recorded and subsequently transcribed. The responses that participants have given will be analysed by the researcher to find out more about what it is like to work in these kinds of situations. All personal information will be removed from the transcript and you will not be identified. Nobody will know that you have taken part, and your anonymity is assured.

**08. How will my data be used?**

> The University of Liverpool processes personal data as part of its research and teaching activities in accordance with the lawful basis of “public task”, and in accordance with the University’s purpose of “advancing education, learning and research for the public benefit”.
>
> Under UK data protection legislation, the University acts as the Data Controller for personal data collected as part of the University’s research. The Dissertation Advisor, Dr. LU LIU, acts as the Data Processor for this study, and any queries relating to the handling of your personal data can be sent to LU LIU at lu.liu@liverpool.ac.uk.
>
> Further information on how your data will be used can be found in the table below.
>
**How will my data be collected?**
>
> Audio-recorded online interview on MS Teams.
>
> The consent form will be distributed and collected via email or social media.
>
**How will my data be stored?**
>
> Recording and anonymised transcripts of the interviews will be saved in the University network storage drive (OneDrive). Anonymised transcripts of the interview will also be stored as a Word document with all identifiers removed, together with electronically signed consent forms in the student researcher’s encrypted personal laptop.
>
**How long will my data be stored for?**
>
> Once the transcript has been completed, the original audio recording will be deleted. Anonymised transcript will be kept until the Master’s dissertation module has been completed by the student and the grade approved by the University, and then for up to 5 years after the completion date to allow the research to be published as a journal or conference paper.
>
**What measures are in place to protect the security and confidentiality of my data?**
>
> Audio recording and anonymised transcripts will be saved on OneDrive. All electronic files will also be kept in secured, password-protected personal laptop that only the student researcher can access.
>
**Will my data be anonymised?**
>
> Yes, the transcript will be fully anonymised and labelled only as “Participant A”, “Participant B”, etc.
>
**How will my data be used?**
>
> It will be analysed along with all of the other interview data collected from other participants, for the purposes of a Master’s student dissertation. The dissertation may also be published as an academic journal or conference paper.
>
**Who will have access to my data?**
>
> Student researcher and Dissertation Advisor, and the dissertation moderator if needed.
>
**Will my data be archived for use in other research projects in the future?**
>
> No. The data will only be used in the dissertation and in research papers developed from the dissertation. The data will not be used in other research projects.
>
**How will my data be destroyed?**
>
> Electronic files will be permanently deleted from all places where they are saved.

**09. Expenses and / or payments**

> There are no financial gains to be expected or any forms of participatory reward in exchange of your participation. You will not incur any expenses and spend money for taking part in this research, either.

**10. Are there any risks in taking part?**

> You will not be exposed to any risks, hazards or adverse effects as a result of your participation in this study. However, in the unlikely event that you experience any discomfort or disadvantage as part of the research, please make this known to the researcher straight away.

**11. Are there any benefits in taking part?**

> There are no immediate benefits in taking part in this study. However, the findings may help knowledge-based firms understand how to sustain collaboration, trust, and knowledge sharing within their teams, and a short summary of the findings will be made available to you.

**12. What will happen to the results of the study?**

> Results of the study will be written up by the student researcher as a Master’s dissertation. The results of the study will also be summarised in a short document that will be sent to all participants for information only. The dissertation may also be published as an academic journal paper within 5 years after the degree is confirmed. You will not be identifiable from the results.

**13. What will happen if I want to stop taking part?**

> You can change your mind at any time and stop participating in the study. You do not have to give any reason. Simply contact the student researcher and let them know. If you withdraw from the study after the interview has taken place, you can ask for your interview recording to be destroyed within a week after the interview. Once transcription has taken place, your interview will be anonymised and it will not be possible to identify it in order to destroy it.

**14. What if I am unhappy or if there is a problem?**

> If you are unhappy, or if there is a problem, please feel free to let us know by contacting the Dissertation Advisor, Dr. LU LIU at lu.liu@liverpool.ac.uk and we will try to help. If you remain unhappy or have a complaint which you feel you cannot come to us with then you should contact the University’s Research Ethics and Integrity Office at ethics@liv.ac.uk. When contacting the Research Ethics and Integrity Office, please provide details of the name or description of the study (so that it can be identified), the researcher(s) involved, and the details of the complaint you wish to make.
>
> The University strives to maintain the highest standards of rigour in the processing of your data. However, if you have any concerns about the way in which the University processes your personal data, it is important that you are aware of your right to lodge a complaint with the Information Commissioner’s Office by calling (+44) 0303 123 1113.
