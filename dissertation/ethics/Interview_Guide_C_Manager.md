**Interview Guide C. Supplementary Participants (managers)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study | Tall Poppy Syndrome as a Management Challenge in Knowledge-Based Firms |
| Version | 1, 4 July 2026 |
| Researcher | Culous Yu, University of Liverpool |

*Note for the reviewer. This supplementary semi-structured guide is for participants who have managed a team that included a standout performer. It explores their observations, the strategies they used, and the gaps they perceived. The questions draw on the participant’s own experience and stay at the professional level, giving balanced room to describe both what eased and what strained teamwork. Section B addresses the conditions that shape the dynamics (RQ1), and Section C addresses the experience of those dynamics, knowledge sharing, and the managerial response (RQ2).*

**Interviewer opening (read to the participant)**

Thank you for taking part. This conversation will take about 45 to 60 minutes, and it is entirely voluntary. You are free to skip any question, to pause, or to stop at any time, you do not need to give a reason, and you may ask for your recording to be destroyed at any time up until I have completed the transcript. There are no right or wrong answers, and I am interested in your own professional experience and observations. Everything you share will be kept confidential and reported without anything that could identify you or your employer. With your consent, I will audio-record the interview through Microsoft Teams so that I can prepare an anonymised transcript. Are you happy for us to begin?

**Section A. Opening**

1. To start, could you tell me a little about your role, the kind of firm you work in, and the team you have managed?
2. Could you describe your experience of managing, or working closely with, a team that included a standout or exceptional performer?

**Section B. Observing the dynamics (RQ1, conditions)**

3. What did you observe about how the team responded to a high-performing member? *(Explore comparisons drawn within the team, and how people responded to standout performance.)*
4. How did you approach recognition, rewards, and visibility for strong performers, and what effects did you notice? *(Draw out what you observed about fairness, and how the team responded.)*
5. How did the way work was structured or shared influence how the team responded to standout performance? *(Consider how interdependent the work was, and workplace norms around standing out.)*

**Section C. Knowledge sharing, cohesion, and managerial response (RQ2, experience)**

6. What did you notice about how knowledge and information were shared in the team during those periods? *(Explore willingness to share, and responsiveness to requests for help.)*
7. What behaviours did you observe around including or leaving out members within the team? *(Consider both how members were brought in, and any informal exclusion.)*
8. How would you describe the trust and cohesion within the team, and what shaped it? *(Consider openness of communication, and any tension or avoidance.)*
9. What strategies, if any, did you use to sustain collaboration and knowledge sharing when these dynamics arose? *(Ask what worked well, and what proved difficult.)*
10. Looking back, where do you see the main gaps or challenges in managing these situations? *(Draw out the support you had or lacked, and anything you would approach differently.)*
11. What effect did these dynamics have on the team’s collective knowledge or performance?

**Section D. Closing**

12. What do you think would help managers to address these challenges?
13. Is there anything else you would like to add?

*Thank the participant, remind them of the right to withdraw before transcription, and confirm how a short summary of the findings will be shared.*
