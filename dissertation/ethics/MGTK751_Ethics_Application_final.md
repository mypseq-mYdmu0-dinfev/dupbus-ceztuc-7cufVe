### Section 3: Description of the research - Aims

**Q3.6.** Please describe the research aims:
> This research explores how the presence of an outstanding performer within a team can change the way colleagues work together, share knowledge, and support one another day to day. Many organisations, including legal, accounting, consulting, engineering, IT, and R&D firms, rely heavily on the specialist expertise of their staff, and depend on people voluntarily sharing what they know with colleagues. When one team member consistently stands out, some studies suggest that colleagues can respond with subtle forms of social distancing or reduced cooperation, sometimes called "Tall Poppy Syndrome". This pattern has been studied in psychology, but little is known about what it means for how organisations manage knowledge and maintain effective teamwork.
>
> This study aims to understand, from the perspective of colleagues who have witnessed it, what conditions make this kind of social friction more or less likely, and how it affects the willingness of team members to share knowledge and collaborate. The findings are intended to help managers recognise these dynamics early and respond in ways that protect both team cohesion and the flow of valuable knowledge within their organisation. The research will also inform the researcher's own consulting practice, which specialises in advising organisations on knowledge management, giving the findings a practical, real-world application beyond the academic contribution.

### Section 3: Description of the research - Design

**Q3.7.** Please describe the research design:
> This is a qualitative study, meaning it is based on people's own words and experiences rather than numbers or statistics. The researcher will conduct one-to-one interviews with at least 6 participants, each currently or previously working in a knowledge-based organisation such as a legal, accounting, consulting, engineering, IT, or R&D firm. No more than one participant will be recruited from any single organisation, so that the findings reflect a pattern seen across different workplaces rather than the culture of one particular employer.
>
> Participants will be asked to reflect on their own professional experience of working alongside a colleague who stood out as a strong performer, and what they observed about how the team responded. Interviews will be semi-structured, meaning the researcher will use a flexible set of open-ended questions as a guide, allowing participants to expand on whatever they feel is most relevant. Each interview will last around 45 to 60 minutes and take place remotely via video call.
>
> Interviews will be audio-recorded, transcribed, and then anonymised. The researcher will analyse the transcripts using thematic analysis, a recognised method for identifying recurring patterns and themes across multiple interviews. Recruitment will continue until no new themes are emerging from the data, which is the standard way qualitative researchers judge that enough interviews have been conducted.
