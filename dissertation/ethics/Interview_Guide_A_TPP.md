**Interview Guide A. Primary Participants (peers of standout performers)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study | Tall Poppy Syndrome as a Management Challenge in Knowledge-Based Firms |
| Version | 1, 4 July 2026 |
| Researcher | Culous Yu, University of Liverpool |

*Note for reviewer: This is a semi-structured guide. The questions below are indicative, and the interviewer may follow them with brief prompts or adapt the wording slightly to the flow of the conversation, whilst keeping to these topics. The questions draw on the participant’s own experience and observations across current or previous roles, and do not ask the participant to evaluate any employer’s policies or practices. Every question stays at the level of professional observation of team behaviour and collaboration rather than personal or emotional disclosure, and each gives balanced room to describe both what eased and what strained teamwork. Section B explores the conditions that shape the dynamics (RQ1), and Section C explores how team members experience them (RQ2).*

**Interviewer opening (read to the participant)**

Thank you for taking part. This conversation will take about 45 to 60 minutes, and it is entirely voluntary. You are free to skip any question, to pause, or to stop at any time, you do not need to give a reason, and you may ask for your recording to be destroyed at any time up until I have completed the transcript. There are no right or wrong answers, and I am interested in your own professional experience and observations. Everything you share will be kept confidential and reported without anything that could identify you or your employer. With your consent, I will audio-record the interview through Microsoft Teams so that I can prepare an anonymised transcript. Are you happy for us to begin?

**Section A. Opening**

1. To start, could you tell me a little about your role and the kind of firm you work in?
2. Thinking of a team you have worked in, could you describe a time when a colleague was widely regarded as a standout or exceptional performer? What was the team like around that time?

**Section B. Working alongside a standout performer (RQ1, conditions)**

3. When a colleague stood out in that way, what did you notice change in how the team worked together? *(Explore any comparisons drawn between colleagues, and any changes you observed in how people paced or aligned their work.)*
4. What did you observe about how recognition, rewards, or visibility were handled in that environment, and what you noticed in how the team responded? *(Draw out what you observed about fairness, and how leaders or managers responded.)*
5. What behaviours did you notice from team members towards a high performer? *(Look for supportive or encouraging behaviours, as well as any distancing or undermining ones.)*
6. How did the way work was shared or divided affect how people responded to a standout colleague? *(Consider whether the work was closely connected or separate, and whether standing out was encouraged or discouraged in that workplace.)*
7. When the team worked well together around a standout performer, what seemed to help? *(Ask about a shared sense of team identity, common goals, and inclusive leadership.)*

**Section C. Team dynamics and knowledge sharing (RQ2, experience)**

8. Thinking about how knowledge and information were shared in the team, did you notice any change in how willingly people shared what they knew? Could you describe what you saw? *(Explore responsiveness to requests for help, and participation in briefings, handovers, or documentation.)*
9. What did you observe about give-and-take in the team, such as sharing expertise or helping one another? *(Look at how readily people shared, and any shift in who helped whom.)*
10. What behaviours did you notice in how team members included or left out one another in that setting? *(Consider both how people were brought into conversations and decisions, and any informal exclusion.)*
11. How would you describe the trust and cohesion within the team during that period, and what did you see that shaped it? *(Consider openness of communication, collaboration across the team, and any tension or avoidance.)*
12. If collaboration or knowledge sharing changed during that period, what did you observe about how that played out for the team’s work and for how knowledge moved around the team? *(Consider both knowledge that moved well, and any that was not passed on.)*

**Section D. Closing**

13. Looking back, what do you think shaped how the team worked around standout performers, and what helped it to work well?
14. Is there anything else about working alongside standout performers, and how it shaped teamwork or knowledge sharing, that you would like to add?

*Thank the participant, remind them of the right to withdraw before transcription, and confirm how a short summary of the findings will be shared.*
