**Interview Guide B. Supplementary Participants (standout performers)**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Study | Tall Poppy Syndrome as a Management Challenge in Knowledge-Based Firms |
| Version | 1, 4 July 2026 |
| Researcher | Culous Yu, University of Liverpool |

*Note for the reviewer. This supplementary semi-structured guide is used only where a participant identifies during the interview as someone who has been regarded as a standout performer. It explores their professional experience of team dynamics and their own approach to sharing knowledge, drawing on their own experience across current or previous roles rather than any evaluation of an employer’s policies. As with Guide A, the questions stay at the professional level, do not ask about personal distress, and give balanced room to describe both what eased and what strained teamwork. Section B addresses the conditions that shape the dynamics (RQ1), and Section C addresses how those dynamics and knowledge sharing are experienced (RQ2).*

**Interviewer opening (read to the participant)**

Thank you for taking part. This conversation will take about 45 to 60 minutes, and it is entirely voluntary. You are free to skip any question, to pause, or to stop at any time, you do not need to give a reason, and you may ask for your recording to be destroyed at any time up until I have completed the transcript. There are no right or wrong answers, and I am interested in your own professional experience and observations. Everything you share will be kept confidential and reported without anything that could identify you or your employer. With your consent, I will audio-record the interview through Microsoft Teams so that I can prepare an anonymised transcript. Are you happy for us to begin?

**Section A. Opening**

1. To start, could you tell me a little about your current or a recent role, and the kind of firm you work in?
2. Could you describe a time when you were recognised as a strong or standout performer within your team? What was the team like around that time?

**Section B. Recognition and team conditions (RQ1, conditions)**

3. When you were recognised in that way, what did you notice change in how colleagues worked with you? *(Explore comparisons within the team, and shifts in everyday interactions.)*
4. What did you observe about how recognition, rewards, or visibility were handled, and how that affected working relationships? *(Draw out what you observed about fairness, and the response of managers or leaders.)*
5. How did the way work was shared or organised shape how the team responded to strong performance? *(Consider whether the work was closely connected or separate, and whether standing out was encouraged or discouraged.)*

**Section C. Knowledge sharing and team dynamics (RQ2, experience)**

6. Thinking about sharing your expertise with the team, how did you approach what you shared, and with whom? *(Explore how openly knowledge was shared, and any selectivity in what was volunteered.)*
7. Were there points where you became more selective about the knowledge you volunteered, and what was happening in the team around those times? *(Look at the circumstances at the time, and give-and-take within the team.)*
8. How would you describe give-and-take, or reciprocity, in how knowledge was shared within the team? *(Ask whether sharing was matched by others, and how this changed over time.)*
9. What did you observe about how the team collaborated, communicated, and included one another during that period? *(Consider cohesion and trust, and any tension or distancing.)*
10. What effect did these dynamics have on how the team’s collective know-how was shared or retained? *(Draw out knowledge passed on or held back, and effort that was duplicated.)*

**Section D. Closing**

11. What do you think organisations could do to support strong performers whilst keeping teams cohesive?
12. Is there anything else you would like to add?

*Thank the participant, remind them of the right to withdraw before transcription, and confirm how a short summary of the findings will be shared.*
