**Completing the risk assessment form**

The key to producing a "suitable and sufficient" risk assessment is to
have a thorough understanding and knowledge of the hazards (i.e.
elements of the work that can cause injury or illness) and to introduce
a range of measures that reduce the risk of injury or ill-health to as
low a level as possible. There are many aspects of a task or activity
that have the potential to cause injury. The tables below list some of
the common issue to think about when planning activities:

Once you have identified the hazards, you need to state what is it about
the hazard that could lead to injury and then state how you intend to
address this issue (i.e. introduce control measures). Some control
measures may already be in place or may be simple to adopt. You should
list these. A second column allows you to think of additional controls,
perhaps by speaking to other members of staff to get their views,
consulting guidance, or speaking to safety professionals. In the second
column you should make note of who is responsible for ensuring controls
are introduced and what the timescales are for introduction. Having
agreed controls, you the need to make an overall risk rating (based on
what actions you have taken (or plan to take) to protect those
involved).

A final section allows you to list the basic procedures that need to be
taken if a critical incident occurs. The form then needs to be signed
off by the assessor and approver. If it is considered necessary to have
a peer review, then the led person of that review group should also sign
off the assessment.
