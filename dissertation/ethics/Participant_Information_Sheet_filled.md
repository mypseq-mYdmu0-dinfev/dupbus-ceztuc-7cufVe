**Participant Information Sheet**

**Title of Study: Tall Poppy Syndrome as a Management Challenge in Knowledge-Based Firms**

**Version and date:** Version 1, 4 July 2026

**Invitation to participate in a research study**

> You are being invited to participate in a research study. Before you
> decide whether to participate, it is important for you to understand
> why the research is being done and what it will involve. Please take
> time to read the following information carefully and feel free to ask
> us if you would like more information or if there is anything that you
> do not understand. Please also feel free to discuss this with your

>

**What is the purpose of the study?**

> This research explores how the presence of standout performers shapes
> team dynamics, collaboration, and knowledge sharing within Australian
> knowledge-based firms, that is, professional firms whose value rests on
> expertise, such as those in law, accounting, consulting, engineering,
> and technology. The findings could inform how these firms sustain
> collaboration and knowledge sharing when individual performance varies.

**Why have I been chosen to take part?**

> You have been invited because you have professional experience of
> working within a knowledge-based firm in Australia, and may have
> observed how a team responds when a colleague stands out as a strong
> performer. Participants are approached individually, and no more than
> one person is recruited from any single organisation.

**Do I have to take part?**

> No. Your participation is entirely voluntary. You can choose whether
> or not to take part. Even if you choose to take part, you can change
> your mind and you are free to withdraw at any time without giving any
> explanation. There will be no negative impact or disadvantage to you
> if you choose not to take part, nor if you change your mind and choose
> to withdraw.

**What will happen if I take part?**

> If you choose to take part in the study then you will be interviewed
> once by the researcher, who is a University of Liverpool Master’s
> student called Culous Yu. You will be asked around 10-15 open ended
> questions by the researcher. The questions will be about your
> experiences of teamwork, collaboration, and knowledge sharing when
> working alongside standout performers. This should take about 45-60
> minutes of your time. The interview will be held online through
> Microsoft Teams and will be audio recorded and subsequently transcribed.
> The responses that participants have given will be analysed by the
> researcher to find out more about what it is like to work in these
> kinds of situations. All personal information will be removed from the
> transcript and you will not be identified. Nobody will know that you
> have taken part, and your anonymity is assured.

How will my data be collected?

Recorded online interview on Microsoft Teams, held through the
researcher’s University of Liverpool account.

The consent form will be distributed and collected via email or social
media.

How will my data be stored?

Recording and anonymised transcription of interview will be uploaded to
the University network storage drive (OneDrive). Transcription of
interview will also be stored as a Word document with all identifiers
removed, together with electronically signed consent forms in the
student researcher’s encrypted personal laptop or PC.

How long will my data be stored for?

Once the transcript has been completed, the original audio recording
will be deleted. The anonymised transcript will be kept until the
Master’s dissertation module has been completed and the grade approved
by the University, and then for up to 5 years after the completion date
to allow the findings to be published as a journal or conference paper.

What measures are in place to protect the security and confidentiality
of my data?

Audio recording will be saved on OneDrive. All electronic files kept in
secured, password-protected personal laptop that only the student
researcher can access. Paper documents, if any, in a locked cabinet.

Will my data be anonymised?

Yes, the transcript will be fully anonymised and labelled only as
‘Participant A, Participant B’ etc.

How will my data be used?

It will be analysed along with all of the other interview data collected
from other participants, for the purposes of a Master’s student
dissertation. The dissertation may also be published as an academic
journal or conference paper within 5 years after the degree is confirmed.

Who will have access to my data?

Student researcher and Dissertation Advisor, and the dissertation
moderator if needed.

Will my data be archived for use in other research projects in the
future?

No. The data will only be used in the dissertation and in research
papers developed from the dissertation. The data will not be used in
other research projects.

How will my data be destroyed?

Electronic files will be deleted. Paper documents will be shredded.

**Are there any risks in taking part?**

> The questions concern your professional observations of teamwork and
> collaboration rather than personal or emotional matters, so the risk to
> you is low. Some people may nonetheless find it uncomfortable to recall
> certain experiences at work. You are free to skip any question, to
> pause, or to stop at any time, and you do not need to give a reason. If
> anything raised leaves you feeling uncomfortable, please tell the
> researcher, who will gladly pause or stop, and you may also wish to
> speak with a trusted colleague, your general practitioner, or a
> confidential support service.

**Are there any benefits in taking part?**

> There are no immediate benefits in taking part in this study. However,
> the findings may help knowledge-based firms understand how to sustain
> collaboration, trust, and knowledge sharing within their teams, and a
> short summary of the findings will be made available to you.

**What will happen to the results of the study?**

> Results of the study will be written up by the student researcher as a
> Master’s dissertation. The results of the study will also be
> summarised in a short document that will be sent to all participants
> for information only. The dissertation may also be published as an
> academic journal paper within 5 years after the degree is confirmed.
> You will not be identifiable from the results.

**What will happen if I want to stop taking part?**

> You can change your mind at any time and stop participating in the
> study. You do not have to give any reason. Simply contact the student
> researcher and let them know. If you withdraw from the study after the
> interview has taken place, you can ask for your interview recording to
> be destroyed at any time up until the transcription has been completed.
> Once transcription has taken place, your interview will be anonymised
> and it will not be possible to identify it in order to destroy it.
